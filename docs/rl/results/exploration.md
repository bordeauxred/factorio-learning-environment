# L2 exploration from scratch

This is a one-seed (`20260914`), fixed-map (`44340`) comparison on Factorio 2.0.73. The L2 reset gives the player one burner mining drill and five coal, with an iron patch and a fixed wooden output chest. The goal remains **four automatically mined iron ore within 16 actions**. Only `WAIT`, `MOVE`, `PLACE`, and `INSERT` are allowed. Both arms use one `open_world` container on **RCON port 27098**, `game.speed=10`, the current 22,817-value observation (including two exact readiness lanes), the same branching Double DQN on MPS, batch size 64, one update per real transition, no demonstrations, no HER, and 80 training episodes (1,280 real transitions). Five fresh greedy checks after each 25 training episodes are excluded from training statistics; final greedy evaluation uses 24 fresh resets. A separate 24-episode masked-random baseline was run with E0. The same seed and map do not estimate reliability across seeds or maps; the current observation is two values wider than in the historical score run.

**The optimized and reported objective is identical in every arm: raw `delta PS`, the change in FLE's automated production score.** The E1 intrinsic score is used only to choose behaviour actions. It is never written to replay, added to a Q target, or included in reported return. The fuel action's observed negative score remains intact; discounted future production can outweigh it.

- **E0 control:** the existing conditional-mask epsilon-greedy `learner.act` decoder. Epsilon falls linearly from 1.0 to 0.1 across the 80 training episode indices. Earlier score runs indexed the same endpoints by elapsed training time; episode indexing here gives matched epsilon exposure at the matched transition budget.
- **E1 count novelty:** on the epsilon exploration branch, sample operations with weight `1/sqrt(1 + N(stage, operation))`. The stage is a four-bit summary of drill held, drill placed, exact receiver-ready fuel state, and whether automatic iron has appeared. For `PLACE`, propose observed iron-ore tile/direction tuples within the existing ±8 offset head and sample with weight `1/sqrt(1 + N(tile, direction))`; other operation arguments use the existing conditional-mask sampler. This uses only cached observation features, is cheap to count, and searches distinct plausible drill placements without a scripted target coordinate. The intrinsic influence is annealed by the shared epsilon schedule. **E1 changes the entire exploration proposal**, including the observed-ore restriction, so any benefit cannot be attributed to the count factor alone.

The main outcomes are episode-level milestones. “On ore” means a successful drill placement with an iron tile in the logged placement tile's 3×3 neighbourhood. This allows one tile of coordinate tolerance around the 2×2 miner area because the action log truncates fractional negative positions; it is a placement-geometry proxy that may overcount, while automatic ore and the game's receiver-alignment flag are exact. “Placed and fueled” additionally requires a successful coal INSERT into that drill. A working drill means at least two automatic ore; the goal is at least four. Distinct attempted placement tiles include rejected attempts; distinct successful tiles count `ok` PLACE actions. All first/second-half p values below are exact two-sided permutation tests for binary outcomes, conditioning on the total number of episodes that met the milestone. There are no smoothed curves or selected block sizes.

E1 substantially improved useful coverage at the matched transition budget. Its on-ore proxy rose from E0's 17/80 to 68/80 (between-arm exact permutation p=1.90×10⁻¹⁶), and working drills rose from 13/80 to 47/80 (p=3.80×10⁻⁸). Total attempted tile breadth was lower, 54 versus 84 distinct tiles, because E1 concentrated on iron. Successful placement breadth was higher, 42 versus 13 distinct tiles; successful tile/direction tuples were 50 versus 16. E1's two goal hits versus E0's zero are encouraging but sparse (between-arm p=0.4969). The on-ore proxy can overcount marginal placements; the automatic-ore and goal comparisons do not rely on it.

The two E1 goal episodes were real, with exact receiver alignment and unchanged score reward. Episode 66 eventually placed west-facing at `(109,-128)`, fueled, and first passed four ore on action 14, ending at 33 automatic ore and +87 `delta PS`. Episode 78 placed east-facing at the scripted `(106,-128)` site, fueled, and first passed four ore on action 3, also ending at 33 ore and +87 score. The first trace shows that this chest has at least one successful layout besides the scripted one. The five greedy checks after episode 50 all made exactly two ore with `PLACE → INSERT`, but after episode 75 all five made zero. These checks are descriptive; they are not in the training fractions.

The saved E1 policy did **not** close L2 from scratch at this budget. It reached **0/24** four-ore goals in fresh greedy evaluation, the same as E0's **0/24** and the fresh random baseline's **0/24** (goal-success permutation p=1 for either policy versus random). It also made zero ore in all 24 final episodes. E1 chose the same `PLACE` at `(101,-135)` on all 384 evaluation actions; Factorio rejected all 384 because the tile was unplaceable. E0's final policy made zero ore through legal but unproductive actions. Neither training arm had a preflight-invalid combination, so this failure is physical action feasibility and policy selection rather than the previously fixed sentinel/decoder bug.

The remaining blocker is now more specific than “the agent never explores.” Ore-focused novelty exposes the place–fuel–run sequence often and even found two receiver-aligned successes, but the factored Q policy did not retain a feasible placement in its greedy choice. The strongest direct post-training evidence points to weak representation or masking of physical placement feasibility: E1 repeated one unplaceable action 384 times. Sample budget and credit propagation remain plausible contributors because only two of 80 E1 training episodes (1,280 transitions) reached an aligned goal. The fixture's restricted action set compounds this: one drill is available and `PICKUP`/`ROTATE` are disallowed, so a successfully placed drill with the wrong drop direction cannot be repaired within an episode. The two successes prove the fixture remains solvable, but this experiment cannot isolate architecture from insufficient aligned samples. It does not justify changing the −3 fuel score or the four-ore goal.
