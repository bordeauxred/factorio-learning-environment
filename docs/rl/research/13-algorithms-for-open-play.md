# Algorithms for open play: retaining discoveries and crossing the drill wall

The present result is not a generic sparse-reward failure. Branching Double DQN with prioritized replay (PER) reliably learns hand-fed furnace lines from an empty world and reaches roughly 1,000--1,600 automated production score (APS) after about 50k environment steps. The specific failure is conversion: gears and drills are occasionally discovered, but no drill has been placed, and the greedy UCB value function scores -1. With only about six fresh transitions per wall-second, the next experiment should concentrate computation around self-discovered frontier states while retaining a strong exploiting data stream. It should not change FLE's reward, replay rewards, TD targets with intrinsic bonuses, or support masks.

## 1. Why the deep rungs are forgotten

The reward is a score difference, so the undiscounted episode return telescopes: \(\sum_t r_t=APS_{256}-APS_0\). A discounted return does not. If consuming material costs \(c\) now and production repays exactly \(c\) after \(k\) decisions, its discounted contribution is \(-c+\gamma^k c<0\). At \(\gamma=0.99\), only 7.7% of a reward 255 decisions away reaches the first state; at 0.999, 77.5% remains. Even a six-action conjunction retains 94.1% versus 99.4%, and smelting delays make the effective gap longer. Thus 0.99 teaches short, repeatedly paying furnace loops more readily than investments whose charge precedes their credit. This is a horizon-induced preference, not reward shaping.

Three-step returns expose a credit only three boundaries earlier per update. Ordinary bootstrapping can eventually propagate it farther (Sutton, 1988, *Machine Learning*), but rare chains are overwritten by many zero-reward and familiar furnace transitions. PER preferentially resamples large TD errors (Schaul et al., 2016, ICLR), yet after those errors shrink it does not preserve a coherent successful sequence. The current milestone archive reserves 25% of a batch, but samples uniformly over every transition in all retained episodes. In a 256-step episode, the few decisions near first gear or drill attainment are diluted by the other steps; rung buckets are not balanced at sampling time. Worse, all eight drill-craft discoveries end before the first positive automated-drill reward. No replay rule using unchanged rewards can make that unseen suffix valuable. It can preserve the prefix, but behavior must first produce a rewarding continuation.

The factorization adds interference. The code chooses all 12 heads from state alone and represents complete-action Q as the value plus the op advantage and the mean of independently chosen argument advantages. An item can therefore be good marginally but wrong for the chosen op or entity; quantity and arguments cannot respond to the sampled op. Branching architectures reduce combinatorics (Tavakoli et al., 2018, AAAI), but this independence is a strong approximation precisely where PLACE-drill-on-ore and INSERT-fuel-into-drill are conjunctive.

The cheapest compliant fix is consequently **frontier-balanced episodic sampling plus frontier-conditioned behavior**. Sample archived episodes uniformly by rung, overweight a narrow window around first rung attainment, and keep the stored n-step transitions, rewards, and discounts byte-for-byte unchanged. At the same time, key behavior counts by `(deepest rung reached, op, primary argument)` so that PLACE and INSERT become novel again after a drill is crafted. This is a sampling and behavior change only. It is preferable to standard self-imitation learning: SIL adds a Monte Carlo-return regression target (Oh et al., 2018, ICML), whereas the permitted analogue here is merely to sample high-return or deep episodes more often. It should improve repeat rate; it cannot by itself value a drill until exploration completes the suffix.

For later propagation experiments, test \(\gamma=0.999\) or 1.0 and 10-step/trace returns against the current \(\gamma=0.99,n=3\). The overnight `n=5, gamma=0.999` arm is not a clean negative result because replay capacity, PER alpha/capping, archive use, epsilon schedule, and target period changed simultaneously, with one seed.

## 2. Algorithms ranked for this setting

| Rank | Method and fit | Expected ladder effect | Cost here and decisive control |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rung-conditioned exploration plus prioritized-episode replay.** Use only real APS deltas; prioritize which episodes/windows are sampled, not their rewards. This combines the empirical discovery advantage of counts with the retention motivation of PER/SIL (Schaul et al., 2016, ICLR; Oh et al., 2018, ICML). | More gear/drill repeats and more PLACE attempts immediately after crafting; modest APS loss if an exploiting worker remains. It cannot invent an unobserved positive suffix. | Low: sampler metadata and a count key. Control: identical learner and behavior with the existing uniform-transition archive.
| 2 | **Go-Explore archive-and-return from agent-discovered states.** Restore the deepest self-discovered state, then make short exploratory excursions (Ecoffet et al., 2021, *Nature*). No scripted state or action sequence is admissible. | Highest near-term probability of turning one drill craft into placement, fuel, and ore: it repeatedly attacks the missing suffix instead of replaying 200-step prefixes. | Medium. Restore is available and cheap, but the production ledger is not restored. Treat restored rollouts as training/exploration, never as comparable episode APS; verify state identity and log a fresh score baseline. Control: the same archive sampled for SGD without restores, at matched fresh actions.
| 3 | **Autoregressive joint action policy/Q.** Select op, then condition each argument's values on op and prior arguments while retaining the existing state-support masks. Beam-search the target argmax if exact enumeration is costly. | Should improve conversion at every conjunctive rung, especially PLACE and entity-item INSERT. It improves execution, not discovery by itself. | Medium: decoder, behavior selection, and Double-DQN target selection change; encoder and replay can remain. Control: branching head with identical replay, exploration, parameter budget, and environment steps; report valid complete-action rate by op.
| 4 | **Multi-step traces: Retrace or Peng's Q(\(\lambda\)).** Retrace is a convergent off-policy return with clipped importance ratios (Munos et al., 2016, NeurIPS); Peng's Q(\(\lambda\)) propagates rewards aggressively but is biased off-policy (Peng and Williams, 1996, ICML). Sequence replay also follows the successful R2D2 recipe (Kapturowski et al., 2019, ICLR). | Faster propagation once a production-completing suffix exists; little effect on the first ever drill placement. | Medium. Store sequences and behavior probabilities. UCB is almost deterministic, so Retrace ratios will often truncate; Peng's Q(\(\lambda\)) or Watkins-style traces may be the practical first test. Control: `n=3` at the same replay samples and updates.
| 5 | **Options over macro operations.** Options formalize temporally extended policies (Sutton, Precup, and Singh, 1999, *Artificial Intelligence*); Option-Critic learns them end-to-end (Bacon et al., 2017, AAAI). | Potentially stabilizes furnace-to-gear-to-drill routines after examples exist. | High relative to evidence. The environment already supplies navigation-absorbing macro-actions, and sparse APS gives option termination little supervision. Do not hand-code a drill option. Control: flat autoregressive policy with matched capacity.
| 6 | **Distributional Q heads.** C51 or quantile regression represents the mixture of failure, consumption loss, and large production return (Bellemare, Dabney, and Munos, 2017, ICML; Dabney et al., 2018, AAAI). | Possibly better risk/return separation and Q stability; unlikely to change first-discovery probability. | Low-to-medium. Control expected-Q Double DQN, same behavior. Judge calibration and ladder conversion, not only loss.
| 7 | **World models or planning.** MuZero learns a planning model without reconstructing observations (Schrittwieser et al., 2020, *Nature*), and Sampled MuZero handles large action spaces (Hubert et al., 2021, ICML). | Shallow real-simulator search from rare archive cells could find a suffix. A learned model might eventually amortize the one-step-per-second simulator. | High and presently premature: sparse data around deep states makes model error worst where planning matters, CPU-only training competes with actors, and real lookahead consumes scarce server steps. Control first: 32-action random/UCB excursions from the same restored states.

Minecraft, Crafter, and NetHack support the emphasis on representation and evaluation, but not a shortcut. Minecraft Video PreTraining succeeds with massive external video (Baker et al., 2022, NeurIPS), which conflicts with the no-demonstration premise. Crafter deliberately rewards a broad achievement tree (Hafner et al., 2022, ICLR), unlike FLE's single production-score delta; its milestone density should not be silently imported. The NetHack Learning Environment stresses long-horizon, procedurally varied evaluation (Küttler et al., 2020, NeurIPS), reinforcing the need for held-out map seeds and sequence memory, not proving that recurrence solves this drill wall.

## 3. Exploration: keep UCB discovery without surrendering exploitation

Global UCB at \(c=1.5\) repeatedly pays for broad novelty even after the useful frontier has moved. Its Q-only evaluation of -1 shows that discovery data did not become an exploiting policy. A single global decay to \(c=0.5\) already improved behavior APS in the continuation log, but the better unit is the frontier: maintain counts per deepest rung, and activate UCB chiefly for the 32--64 actions following a new deepest rung. This makes PLACE-drill novel after CRAFT-drill even if PLACE was exhausted in empty or furnace states.

Optimistic initialization is less attractive than counts. In a neural Q function, optimism generalizes unpredictably across thousands of factored tuples, and randomized priors/Bootstrapped DQN already underperformed plain DQN on greedy APS (Osband et al., 2016, NeurIPS; Osband et al., 2018, NeurIPS). Use optimism only for unseen frontier pairs, capped in the behavior score and never stored as reward.

The practical hybrid is two behavior streams sharing one network and replay: one explorer uses rung-conditioned UCB; one exploiter uses epsilon-greedy. Both write ordinary transitions, and the common learner trains only on APS deltas. This separates data acquisition from the greedy policy without maintaining two value functions. On six servers for eight hours, I would run three two-server arms described in Section 5. Within each hybrid arm, one server explores and one exploits. This is a screening experiment, not enough replication for a publication claim.

## 4. The reward signal as given

For the stated objective, “final APS at step 256,” \(\gamma=1\) is mathematically exact because rewards telescope. A value below one changes the objective toward early production and can make temporarily loss-making investments unattractive. In practice, \(\gamma=0.999\) is a conservative first approximation: it retains most of the 256-step horizon while avoiding an immediate simultaneous change to undiscounted targets. The correct comparison is matched `gamma=0.99` versus `0.999` versus `1.0`, with target magnitudes and instability reported.

Increasing to 512 macro steps makes capital investment more valuable and gives a late drill time to repay its setup, but it defines a different task and halves episode count at fixed actions. A true-tick budget is cleaner because MOVE, WAIT, crafting, and tool execution advance unequal simulation time; it prevents a policy from obtaining a different economic horizon merely by choosing different macros. Report both actions and true ticks. Never compare final APS across unequal horizons without also reporting APS per fixed true-tick window.

The four identical greedy episodes in the overnight evaluation are one deterministic trajectory repeated four times. Fair evaluation requires variation, not more replicas: freeze at least 16 held-out map seeds, run one deterministic greedy episode per checkpoint and seed, and report median, interquartile range, mean with a bootstrap interval, ladder attainment, and time-to-first production. Use the same seed list for every arm. Also run a small \(\epsilon=0.02\) evaluation to distinguish a brittle deterministic argmax from a policy with a robust basin. Training-randomness claims ultimately need at least three independent learner seeds; the next eight-hour run can only screen candidates.

## 5. Concrete three-arm plan for the next eight hours

Allocate two servers per arm, stop at 57,600 fresh environment steps per arm or eight hours, and keep the map seed, 256-step horizon, CPU threads, one update per fresh step, batch 64, Adam \(10^{-4}\), replay 100k, PER \(\alpha=0.6\), \(\beta:0.4\rightarrow1\), `n=3`, \(\gamma=0.99\), target copy every 1,000 updates, and raw APS rewards identical. Keeping the proven value-learning settings isolates acquisition and sampling; test gamma/traces only after a suffix is observed.

1. **A -- exploitation baseline:** plain branching Double DQN; epsilon 1.0 to 0.1 over 18,000 steps, then 0.1; existing 25% archive sampler. This anchors score and detects regression.
2. **B -- shared explorer/exploiter:** the same learner and existing archive. Server B1 uses epsilon-greedy, 0.20 to 0.05 over 5,000 steps then 0.05. Server B2 uses counts keyed by `(deepest rung, op, primary)`, UCB \(c\) linearly 0.75 to 0.15 over 40,000 steps, then 0.15, plus epsilon 0.02. Force an unseen pair at most once per rung.
3. **C -- hybrid plus frontier replay/return:** use B's two behaviors. Draw 32 of each 64-sample batch from ordinary PER and 32 from the archive. Of archive samples, 16 come from the deepest available rung, eight from the preceding rung, and eight uniformly from other rungs; choose episodes uniformly, then choose 75% of transitions from steps -32 through +8 around first attainment and 25% from the whole episode. Rewards, n-step discounts, and TD formula remain unchanged. After any self-discovered drill craft, use half of explorer episodes as restored excursions from that state, capped at the smaller of 64 actions and the original episode's remaining budget. Preserve the original step counter. Restored episodes are excluded from APS comparisons and separately labelled because the production ledger restarts. If frontier inventory/world identity fails, disable only the return component and retain the sampler.

The primary decision metric is **root-from-empty episodes in which a burner drill is placed on ore, fuelled, and produces at least one automated ore**, followed by repeat rate after first root success. Precommit to advancing an arm only if it records at least five such episodes, repeat rate at least 0.20, and held-out greedy median APS at least 80% of Arm A. Restored-suffix successes are diagnostics, not wins. If no arm completes the rung from the root, rank by root drill-placement count and the conditional conversion rates `plates -> gear -> drill -> placed`, not by first discovery alone. Break ties with median APS across the 16 held-out map seeds. Log archive draws by rung and offset, explorer versus exploiter provenance, restored versus root episodes, and true ticks; without those diagnostics, another “25% archive” result will remain uninterpretable.
