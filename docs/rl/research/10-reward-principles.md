# Reward principles for the L2 production task

This is a design analysis, not a change to the task or reward code. Keep L2's assigned goal at **four automatically mined iron ore in 16 actions**. The scripted solution reaches 15 ore, so four is feasible on the existing fixture. Calling two ore a success would only rename the observed failure (`docs/rl/results/ladder.md:117-125`; `docs/rl/results/production_score.md:136-146`).

## What failed

**Reward misspecification is a real risk, but it is not the sole or best-supported explanation for L2.** The implemented goal reward is a binary first crossing of exact episode-relative automatic counts. It pays zero for an unsuccessful two-ore trace, even though that trace can generate positive hindsight labels (`fle/rl/reward.py:21-25`; `docs/rl/DESIGN.md:85-91`; `docs/rl/results/production_score.md:63-72`). Net `automated_score` instead charges the fuel action and rewards produced ore: the live `PLACE`, `INSERT`, 60-second `WAIT` sequence changed score by 0, −3, +40 and yielded 15 automatic ore. The alternative gross positive-production reward removes the fuel charge (`docs/rl/results/production_score.md:19-44`). These objectives have different incentives. Yet a denser score reward raised training output from 0.295 to 0.784 ore per episode, with visible partial-production learning, while positive production reached 0.875. The supplied rolling curve rises 0.30 → 0.60 → 1.10 → 1.29, but its binning is **UNVERIFIED** in the written results; the reported episode blocks are 0.320 → 1.120 → 0.917, so those blocks themselves are not monotone (`docs/rl/results/production_score.md:76-85`). All three arms had zero real L2 successes in their matched, single-seed training windows: 0/88, 0/74 and 0/80 (`docs/rl/results/production_score.md:54-67,74-85,101-116`). Density therefore helps some credit assignment; the common zero-success outcome limits the claim that sparse reward alone caused failure. Equal wall-time windows produced different numbers of episodes, so these figures are descriptive, not a controlled sample-efficiency estimate.

**Exploration of a joint geometric dependency is the binding observed obstacle.** The fresh random baseline was 0/24. The final greedy policies under sparse/HER, net score and positive production all placed and fueled a drill, produced exactly two ore per episode, and failed to feed the receiver. The score policy had learned a partial `PLACE → INSERT → WAIT` sequence, but put the drill in the wrong place and orientation; the positive-only reward produced the same stall without the fuel penalty (`docs/rl/results/production_score.md:48-52,67-72,87-95,110-116,118-146`). In a separate L2 run, 32 demonstrated `PLACE → INSERT → WAIT` traces seeded 96 real transitions; the resulting policy reached the unchanged four-ore goal in 32/32 fresh greedy episodes, with 15 ore each. That is strong evidence that exposing the successful joint sequence changes learning on this fixed map, though it does not isolate demonstration coverage from its replay and optimization effects or establish multi-seed transfer (`docs/rl/results/ladder.md:194-215,218-230`).

**Credit assignment and representation remain contributing, unresolved limits.** Reward from either production scalar arrives only after placement and fueling; net score first assigns −3 to fuel. Sparse first-hit gives no assigned-goal reward before four ore, although its partial traces made 57 immediately positive HER copies among 387; HER cannot relabel an unreached four-ore outcome into a real success (`docs/rl/results/production_score.md:63-72,136-138`). The repaired DQN decoder reduced preflight-invalid combinations from the older 40.07% to 0% in all three matched training arms, so that particular decoder bug is no longer binding. Factorio still rejected about 18% of non-WAIT training actions, and the factorized heads allow legal but unproductive drill geometries. The experiment does not separate poor spatial representation, action prior and exploration probability (`docs/rl/results/production_score.md:120-125,148-159`). The separate PPO score run reached 0/148 training success and 0.169 mean automatic ore; its final greedy policy repeated an unplaceable `PLACE`, so that run additionally has a decoder/representation failure and is not evidence that net score cannot work with a valid action model (`docs/rl/results/ppo_production.md:1-11,104-110,128-154`).

## The two-ore stall is a real jam inside a constructed fixture

A burner mining drill has a directional output drop tile. It can mine into its limited output buffer, then reports `waiting_for_space_in_destination` when it cannot unload. L0's live setup explicitly needed a receiving chest to avoid stopping at two ore (`docs/rl/results/ladder.md:13-23`). The L2 reset code chooses a legal drill location on ore, places a chest at `(x+1.5,y-0.5)`, and gives the agent one drill and five coal (`tests/benchmarks/run_ladder.py:40-64`). L2 allows only `WAIT`, `MOVE`, `PLACE` and `INSERT`, so after reset the learner cannot move the chest or rotate/recover a poor placement (`tests/benchmarks/run_ladder.py:24-28`). The scripted east-facing drill at `(106,-128)` delivered into that chest and made 15 ore; the learned drills elsewhere jammed at two (`docs/rl/results/ladder.md:117-125`; `docs/rl/results/production_score.md:136-146`).

Routing output to some receiver is genuine Factorio work. Aligning with **this fixed chest** is a fixture-specific constraint, not an intrinsic requirement for mining four ore in an unrestricted Factorio world. The L2 fixture is physically solvable and its four-ore criterion is valid; the short task description is incomplete if it suggests that any placement on ore plus fuel suffices. Make the chest, drill drop direction and output blockage explicit in the task specification and observations. A different chest position or action set may be a useful *new* curriculum task, but its result cannot be reported as success on this L2 fixture. Whether other layouts could reach four within 16 actions under these exact restricted controls is **UNVERIFIED**; the one scripted layout proves feasibility, not uniqueness.

## A policy-invariant way to add local guidance

Ng, Harada and Russell (1999) give the discounted potential form. For the actual transition discount `β_t = 0.99^((tick_{t+1}-tick_t)/300)`, use

```text
r'_t(s,a,s',g) = r_base(s,a,s',g) + β_t Φ(s',g) − Φ(s,g).
```

This must use the same true-tick `β_t` as the return (`fle/rl/reward.py:28-36`; `docs/rl/DESIGN.md:93-93`). The earlier recipe-DAG proposal put bounded stock and production-rate terms on target ancestors. It was reasonable for longer crafting chains, but stored manual ore could raise it without automation, and its rate term needs window history. It should not be restored unchanged for L2 (`docs/rl/research/03-reward-task.md:58-73,81-95`; `docs/rl/DESIGN.md:95-95`). For L2 define two **exact** predicates on the full action-boundary state. `P(s)` means a placed burner drill overlaps iron ore and its oriented drop tile enters a free receiving chest. `F(s)` means `P(s)` and that drill has usable fuel. Set `Φ_L2(s,g)=½P(s)+½F(s)` for the unchanged iron-ore goal, and zero for other goal items unless their own exact, goal-relevant readiness predicates are defined. The two halves simply split one unit of goal-success value across the two necessary readiness facts; they are not fitted to a learning curve. `P` or `F` must fall when the receiver blocks, fuel runs out, or the drill is removed. This is a state potential, not a bonus paid every time an action is named `PLACE` or `INSERT`.

Under the binary first-hit base, a successful nonterminal L2 path with `Φ` equal to `0, ½, 1, 1` at reset, after `PLACE`, after `INSERT`, and after `WAIT` has immediate shaped rewards `β_PLACE/2`, `β_INSERT−1/2`, and `1+β_WAIT−1=β_WAIT`. A nominal 60-game-second wait is 3,600 ticks and gives `β_WAIT=0.99^12≈0.8864`; actual place/insert tick gaps must be read, so their numerical `β` values are **UNVERIFIED** (`docs/rl/results/production_score.md:21-32`; `docs/rl/DESIGN.md:93-93`). Correct placement and fueling now receive local positive feedback for ordinary short transitions, while the discounted sum of all shaping terms cancels at the terminal boundary. The potential does not create a four-ore success from a two-ore trace.

The same construction can make fueling **locally positive even under net score** without forgiving consumed coal. Use `Φ_score(s,g)=20P(s)+20F(s)` for this L2 illustration, with the same terminal rule. Forty score units is the observed output gain of the scripted 60-second continuation, split across the two documented prerequisites; it is not a searched reward weight or an inferred per-ore price from a floored scalar (`docs/rl/results/production_score.md:21-36`). The sequence then gives `PLACE: 20β_PLACE`, `INSERT: −3+40β_INSERT−20 = 40β_INSERT−23`, and, if the drill stays fueled and aligned, `WAIT: +40+40β_WAIT−40 = 40β_WAIT`. Fueling is locally positive whenever `β_INSERT>0.575`. No bounded potential can guarantee a positive fuel step for arbitrarily long discounted transitions, and the theorem promises policy invariance, not that every step becomes positive. This score example explains the mechanism; it does not require changing the official score or adopting it as L2's base objective.

The guarantee is precise. For a trajectory of `T` transitions, shaping sums to `−Φ(s_0,g)+(∏_{t<T}β_t)Φ(s_T,g)`. In the finite 16-action L2 episode, the second term would reward ending with a fueled drill unless `Φ(terminal,g)=0` or an equivalent boundary correction is paid on the last transition. Goal hits do not terminate the current episodes, so a hit alone must not erase the potential; actual truncation does (`docs/rl/DESIGN.md:91-97`; `docs/rl/research/03-reward-task.md:81-83`). The initial state and goal must be fixed for the compared policies. The usual Markov optimal-policy theorem also requires a potential of the Markov state. An exact, consistent potential still telescopes pathwise under partial observation, but a stale or inconsistent estimate does not implement that potential. A trailing production rate is not a function of an instantaneous factory snapshot: its tick-stamped history or exact window accumulator must be included in state and replay before it can be used this way (`docs/rl/research/03-reward-task.md:75-83`; `docs/rl/DESIGN.md:95-99`). The current selected and quantized entity cache cannot certify full-force output geometry, receiver capacity and fuel in both stored HER states. Exact, fresh, goal-conditioned readiness lanes and offline replay recomputation are therefore prerequisites, as the design already identified (`docs/rl/DESIGN.md:57-63,95-99`). Do not silently compute this potential from selected stale rows.

## Choose the base objective before shaping it

For the **current L2 and open-track learning curriculum**, retain the exact goal-conditioned first-hit objective. It encodes the assigned task, is recomputable for future HER goals from stored automatic counts, and preserves the real four-ore success test (`fle/rl/reward.py:21-25`; `docs/rl/DESIGN.md:85-101`). Its cost is that a one-time threshold does not demand sustained output: a four-ore line may stop after the hit. That is a task definition issue to expose with a separate rate holdout, not a reason to award a two-ore stall. A later throughput task should use its own explicit target item, window and threshold. Existing named FLE throughput tasks measure automatic output in nominal 60-second windows, whereas open play *describes* a score rate but Inspect actually reports final automated score (`docs/rl/research/03-reward-task.md:8-9,75-79`; `docs/rl/research/05-open-track-goals.md:5-11`). These are distinct evaluation contracts.

FLE's `automated_score` is the right **benchmark metric** to report alongside success. It prices each item's production minus consumption, removes recorded manual harvest and net manual crafting, and floors the scalar (`fle/env/tools/agent/score/server.lua:309-330,335-376,391-408`; `docs/rl/research/05-open-track-goals.md:15-23`). Its delta is a coherent base objective if the declared task is to maximize FLE's final economic score, but with `β_t<1` the discounted sum of deltas is not exactly the final score: it also values when gains and costs occur. Score has no item-specific goal to relabel, and its price-weighted scalar cannot recover a target's automatic ore count. HER would require a separate stored goal-conditioned achievement objective, or a multi-objective learner carrying both goal and score with an explicit rule for trading them off. That adds replay, target and evaluation complexity and changes the optimized objective. Keep both measurements; use the goal objective for HER training and automated score as an independent benchmark outcome. Do not add an arbitrary score bonus to the goal reward.

Net consumption accounting should remain in the benchmark score. Charging coal and recipe inputs reflects the value of resources used and discourages fuel waste; removing those charges or switching to gross positive production can reward wasteful loops. Its local −3 fuel step is a genuine delayed-credit problem, not proof that the net accounting is wrong. The positive-only arm removed that penalty and still stalled at two ore (`docs/rl/results/production_score.md:19-44,101-116`). If training directly on score, use a properly terminated potential to move value toward productive preparation, rather than editing FLE's economic metric. In the recommended goal objective the −3 is not in the base reward at all; the readiness potential addresses the zero-feedback precursor chain without changing what counts as success.

## Next experiment and falsification

Use **the unchanged four-ore first-hit reward plus the exact L2 readiness potential**:

```text
r'_t = 1[A_iron(s)<4≤A_iron(s')] + β_t(½P(s')+½F(s')) − (½P(s)+½F(s)),
Φ(terminal)=0,  β_t=0.99^((tick_{t+1}−tick_t)/300).
```

Keep the four-ore goal and 16-action fixture fixed. Record exact `P` and `F` at every boundary, including replay states and terminal truncation; reject a trace if they cannot be established. Recompute original and HER-relabeled rewards from stored states. For other goals, use only a matching goal-specific potential with equally exact facts, or zero potential. This is principled because the base success event stays fixed and the added term telescopes, not because the two half-unit increments are expected to solve exploration. The potential may speed learning but cannot make a missing aligned placement appear in replay.

Address exploration separately, in this order: seed replay with successful L2 demonstrations, since 96 demonstrated transitions already changed the fixed-map result from 0/24 to 32/32; then advance a curriculum from fueled drill to place/fuel/output routing while preserving each rung's named evaluation; then add geometry-aware action priors or conditional candidate selection for drop-tile alignment. Test count-based or other intrinsic exploration only if diverse feasible placements remain rare after those steps, and keep intrinsic reward out of the reported task return (`docs/rl/results/ladder.md:13-30,194-230`; `docs/rl/results/production_score.md:148-159`).

Two measurements can refute the recommendation. First, on every complete stored episode, the discounted shaped-minus-base return must equal `−Φ(s_0,g)` within numerical precision; any residual exposes a discount, terminal or replay-state error, not a valid shaping result. Second, compare real assigned-goal success and receiver-aligned placement on fresh, prespecified feasible L2 layouts and multiple seeds against an otherwise identical demonstration-seeded sparse-reward control. If alignment and real success do not improve at matched environment transitions, the potential has no demonstrated practical benefit; retain the simpler sparse objective and work on exploration or spatial action representation. Report the original four-ore success, ore count, jam status, automated score and final 60-second automatic output separately. HER-labelled success and a two-ore plateau are never substitutes for real task success (`docs/rl/results/production_score.md:118-159`; `docs/rl/research/05-open-track-goals.md:15-23`).
